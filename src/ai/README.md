# Astra engine

The interface creates one module worker from `worker.ts`. It sends `SearchRequest`
messages and discards replies whose request ID, session ID, or position revision
is no longer current. Scores are from **Dark's** perspective, with roughly 100
units per man. They are estimates, not winning percentages.

`SearchEngine` searches complete turns generated by the shared rules engine.
Mutable make/unmake operations also come from that engine and are covered by
the core's differential tests. The search never inspects the DOM.

Search uses iterative deepening, principal-variation alpha-beta, aspiration
windows, capture quiescence, transposition ordering, killer moves and a history
heuristic. Evaluation includes material, advancement, promotion prospects,
center control, back-rank defense, mobility, support, exposure, trapped kings
and conversion of a material lead in endings.

Explicit analysis scores every root alternative with a full window and publishes
the best three lines from the last completed depth. Those scores are exact for
that search horizon, not fail-low/fail-high bounds. Ordinary play and hints retain
single-principal-variation search for speed.

The DFS is a resumable generator. It yields every 64 nodes; the asynchronous
driver gives the worker event loop a turn after approximately 8 ms. Stop,
superseding requests and Play now can therefore interrupt an unfinished depth.
The published move always comes from a completed iteration, or a legal fallback
when an exceptionally short budget does not finish depth 1. Cancellation publishes
no final result. Priority is move > analysis/hint > ponder.

The transposition table uses a fixed 5 MiB allocation. Its two hashes include the
board, moving side, mandatory-capture setting, no-progress count and the entire
repetition-count context. Mate values are adjusted by search ply when stored.
Ponder searches reuse the same table; entries for the actual reply become useful
when a subsequent move request reaches those positions.

Beginner and Casual select reproducibly among alternatives within 120 and 35
units of the best searched move, respectively. Challenging and Expert always use
the best completed result. Nominal budgets are 100 ms, 300 ms, 1 second and
2 seconds; untimed Expert can use 10 seconds. Timed budgets reserve clock time.

`public/data/opening.json` contains 128 original depth-4 opening recommendations.
`public/data/endgame.json` contains 1,024 original depth-6 recommendations for
sparse two- and three-piece positions. These two datasets only guide ordering.
`public/data/two-piece.json` is an **exhaustive original WDL/DTM tablebase** of all
13,952 legal one-piece-versus-one positions: men and kings, both moving sides and
both capture settings. Retrograde analysis gives shortest forced wins and longest
resistance to forced losses; unresolved cyclic components are draws. All data is
generated reproducibly by `npm run generate:knowledge` and totals about 303 KB.

An exact win/loss may be used only when the live no-progress allowance exceeds
the distance to terminal and no historical position has occurred twice. The
strictly decreasing DTM strategy then cannot cause a new repetition, or exhaust
the draw allowance. Otherwise, the legal recommendation is only move ordering
guidance and normal history-aware search determines the score. Tablebase draws
never replace live adjudication. Three-piece coverage remains **sparse finite-depth
guidance, not a complete tablebase**. Data loads on demand; missing data never
prevents play. Subdirectory deployments resolve data relative to the worker's
actual asset URL.

`npm run benchmark` checks shallow minimax agreement and tactical fixtures, then
plays twenty games with paired colors against a corrected legacy baseline with
equal 100 ms per-move budgets. The ten openings are reproducibly seeded and range
from four to twelve plies. The baseline retains the original material/advancement/center/
distance heuristic (historical `source/AI.js` on `main` at `50b6935`) and plain alpha-beta, with full-turn rules and iterative
deepening added for correct, equally timed comparison. Raw measurements,
hardware, methodology and unresolved games are recorded in
`scripts/benchmark-results.json`. It is a small regression sample, not an Elo
rating. `BENCH_BUDGET_MS`, `BENCH_PAIRS`, and `BENCH_PLY_CAP` adjust the run.
